## Blvckshell — Facilities Execution and Accountability Operator

---

## Overview of Ontario Multi-Residential Facilities Services

The market consists of property managers, condominium corporations, and building owners who contract for common-area cleaning, unit turnovers, turnover readiness, light (non-licensed) maintenance, and facilities coordination. Demand is recurring and contract-based for cleaning; turnover and maintenance are recurring or project-based. There is no single official statistic for "multi-residential facilities support" as a distinct segment; estimates below are conservative and derived from building counts, typical contract values, and industry norms. Blvckshell addresses the friction layer between cleaning-only vendors and licensed trades.

## Fragmentation Analysis

- **Supply side:** Many small and mid-size local operators; a smaller number of regional or national chains. No single player dominates Ontario outside major metros.
- **Implications:** Pricing and service quality vary widely. Consistent quality, documentation, and contract discipline can differentiate. Fragmentation also means no single competitor can block entry.
- **Weakness patterns:** Many competitors exhibit one or more of: inconsistent QA, weak documentation, reactive reclean handling, poor billing discipline, or thin insurance/compliance. These create openings for a disciplined operator.

## Labor Market Considerations in Ontario

- **Minimum wage:** Ontario minimum wage applies; total labour cost includes payroll burden (CPP, EI, vacation, etc.) or subcontractor markup.
- **Subcontractor use:** Common in commercial cleaning; reduces fixed payroll but requires margin retention (payout ceiling) and quality control.
- **Availability:** In smaller cities, labour availability can be tighter than in Toronto; wage and travel expectations may differ. Recruitment and retention are operational risks.
- **No assumption:** The plan does not assume access to below-market labour; margins are tested at realistic subcontractor and labour costs.

## Regulatory Environment

| Requirement | Application |
|-------------|-------------|
| **WSIB** | Mandatory in Ontario for applicable workers. Coverage and reporting are maintained from day one. |
| **HST** | Cleaning services are generally taxable. HST is collected and remitted; contracts are clear on tax-inclusive or tax-extra pricing. |
| **Insurance** | Commercial general liability and other coverages as required by clients (e.g. $2M–$5M). Certificates provided; limits and renewals tracked. |
| **Contract law** | Ontario law governs contracts. Terms cover scope, frequency, pricing, termination, and liability. |
