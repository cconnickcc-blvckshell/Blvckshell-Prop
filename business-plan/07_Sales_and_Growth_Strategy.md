# Sales and Growth Strategy

## Blvckshell — Facilities Execution and Accountability Operator

---

## Strategy Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| Contract-first | No expansion into a new city or large commitment without a signed contract or firm commitment. |
| Density before geography | Windsor (launch city) is built to a stable base (e.g. 2–4 sites) before pursuing a second city. |
| Margin discipline | No contract accepted below 20% gross margin (15% floor only under documented stress). Add-ons at 30%+ margin. |
| No speculative hiring | Sales and operations capacity are added when pipeline or signed work justifies them. |

## Expansion Doctrine (Consolidated)

Single reference for geographic and capacity expansion; no deviation without documented exception.

| Rule | Application |
|------|-------------|
| **Density before geography** | Windsor (and any first city) is built to a stable base (e.g. 2–4 sites) before opening a second city. No new city until the launch city is at sustainable density. |
| **Contract trigger** | A new city is entered only when there is a signed contract or firm commitment. No speculative entry. |
| **Capacity-led** | Hiring and subcontractor onboarding follow confirmed or near-confirmed work. No speculative hiring. |
| **No national narrative** | The plan is Ontario regional only; no implied expansion beyond the province in the planning horizon. |
| **Second city** | Entered only when (a) at least one signed contract or firm commitment in that city, and (b) existing city is stable at 4+ sites or revenue supports added complexity. |
| **Third and fourth cities** | Same logic; revenue and capacity must support management and travel/coordination. |

## Pilot Program Strategy and Conversion Framework

Pilots reduce friction in vendor switching: limited scope, fixed price, and clear success criteria. They are pipeline tools to convert to recurring or repeat work, not a substitute for disciplined revenue projection.

### Why Pilots Reduce Friction

- **Low commitment:** Client can test quality and fit without a long-term contract.
- **Scope-locked:** Boundaries are fixed; no creep. Success is measurable.
- **Prepaid/deposit:** Cash flow protection for Blvckshell; no net-30 on pilot work.
- **Conversion path:** Each pilot type has a defined path to recurring cleaning, maintenance retainer, or turnover work.

### Prepaid and Scope-Locked Rules

| Rule | Application |
|------|-------------|
| Prepaid or deposit | Pilot work is prepaid or 50%+ deposit before start. No net-30 for pilots. |
| Scope-locked | Written scope; no out-of-scope work without change order and additional payment. |
| Conversion trigger | Conversion to recurring is a separate contract event; not assumed until signed. |

### Five Pilot Types

#### 1. Unit Turnover Pilot

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Scope | 1 vacant unit; full turnover clean; visual inspection; up to 2 hours light touch-up (non-licensed). |
| Conversion | Per-unit turnover pricing; ongoing maintenance or cleaning if client expands. |
| Revenue | Fixed fee per pilot; margin target 30–40%. |

#### 2. Problem Building Cleaning Pilot

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Scope | 1 building; common areas; garbage + stairwells; weekly inspection report. |
| Conversion | Monthly recurring cleaning contract. |
| Revenue | Fixed pilot fee; margin target 30–40%. |

#### 3. Light Maintenance and Punch-List Pilot

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Scope | 6–10 labour hours; minor repairs only (non-licensed, non-permit). |
| Conversion | Hourly maintenance or monthly retainer. |
| Revenue | Fixed-scope fee; margin target 30–40%. |

#### 4. Turnover Readiness Audit Pilot

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Scope | 1–3 vacant units; inspection only; photo report. |
| Conversion | Cleaning (Tier 1/2), maintenance (Tier 4), or ongoing inspections. |
| Revenue | Per-unit or per-audit fee; margin target 30–40%. |

#### 5. Building Reset / Seasonal Refresh Pilot

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Scope | Pressure washing; carpet refresh; light maintenance; condition report. |
| Conversion | Recurring cleaning; seasonal maintenance contract. |
| Revenue | Fixed-scope fee; margin target 30–40%. |

### Financial Modeling of Pilots

| Item | Assumption |
|------|------------|
| Pilot revenue category | Tracked separately from recurring cleaning, turnover, and maintenance. |
| Pilot gross margin | 30–40% (conservative 30%). |
| Conversion rate | Conservative 30–50% of pilots convert to recurring or repeat work within 12 months. No assumption that 100% convert. |
| Pilot-to-recurring logic | Pilot revenue is not inflated in Year 1. Pilots feed pipeline; recurring revenue is recognized when contract is signed. Forecast uses blended ramp: early revenue may include one-time/pilot; recurring share grows as conversions occur. |

Pilots do not inflate Year 1 revenue unrealistically. They are pipeline tools; conversion is modeled conservatively.

## Target Customer

- **Primary:** Property management companies and condominium corporations that outsource common-area cleaning in Ontario, starting in Windsor.
- **Secondary:** Building owners who self-manage and contract cleaning directly.
- **Decision makers:** Property managers, board members, or designated staff; sales process addresses their priorities (reliability, scope clarity, documentation, price).

---

## Sales Process (High Level)

1. **Lead generation:** Outbound outreach (email, phone, referral), and inbound from referrals or existing relationships. No assumption of large marketing spend in Year 1.
2. **Qualification:** Confirm building type, current arrangement (in-house vs. outsourced), contract timing, and budget range. Disqualify if margin cannot be achieved or if scope is out of focus.
3. **Proposal:** Written proposal with scope, frequency, price, and terms. No verbal-only commitments for new clients.
4. **Negotiation:** Within margin guardrails. If client demands push below 20% gross margin, walk away or re-scope.
5. **Contract:** Signed agreement before service starts. No "trial" at below-margin pricing unless explicitly time-limited and documented.
6. **Onboarding:** Site schedule, checklist, access, and first visits scheduled. Handoff to operations.

## Growth Model (No Hockey Stick)

| Period | Sites (buildings) | Revenue assumption | Comment |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|---------|
| Months 1–3 | 0–1 | Low or zero | Sales and setup; first contract may close in Month 2–3. |
| Months 4–6 | 1–2 | Ramp begins | First site(s) live; second site possible. |
| Months 7–12 | 2–4 | $8K–$18K/month run rate by Month 12 | Density in one city only. |
| Year 2 | 4–10 | Growth from retention and 1–2 new cities | Second city only when revenue and capacity support it. |
| Year 3 | 8–18 | Further density and selective new cities | Management layer and systems scale with size. |

Growth is slow and stepped. No assumption of rapid multi-city rollout or large contract wins in Year 1.

---

## Retention and Renewal

- **Contract term:** Typically 12 months with renewal; renewal is a sales event (proposal, negotiation, signature).
- **Retention levers:** Reliable delivery, clear scope, documentation, responsive reclean and incident handling. Price is one factor among several.
- **Churn assumption:** Conservative planning allows for non-renewal or early exit on 1–2 sites over 12–24 months; not all clients renew.

---

## Referral and Reputation

- Referrals from property managers or boards are a target source; no quantified assumption in the financial model.
- Reputation is built through execution: on-time visits, consistent quality, clear communication. No paid review or testimonial strategy in Year 1.

---

## Marketing and Brand

- **Spend:** Minimal in Year 1; no large ad budgets or speculative campaigns.
- **Materials:** Professional one-pagers, proposal template, and contract template; factual, no hype.
- **Digital:** Basic web presence and contact capability; no assumption that web alone drives material lead volume in Year 1.

---

## Summary

Sales and growth are contract-led and margin-disciplined. Pilot programs provide a low-friction entry for new clients; conversion to recurring contracts is modeled conservatively (30–50%). Launch is Windsor; second city follows only when justified by contract and capacity. No hockey-stick revenue assumption; growth is stepped and conservative.
