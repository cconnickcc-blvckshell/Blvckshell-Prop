# Break-Even and Sensitivity Analysis

## Blvckshell — Ontario Multi-Residential Service Operator

---

## Break-Even Without Founder Salary

**Fixed costs (Year 1):** $8,400 (overhead only; no founder salary).

**Contribution per dollar of revenue:** At 25% gross margin, each $1 of revenue contributes $0.25 to gross profit.

**Break-even revenue:** $8,400 / 0.25 = **$33,600** annual revenue, or **$2,800 per month** sustained.

**In site terms:** At $2,800 per site per month, **one site** at target margin breaks even on overhead. So with 0 sites, the company is below break-even; with 1 site at $2,800/month and 25% margin, the company is at break-even (no profit, no loss). With 2+ sites, net profit is positive (before any one-time or timing effects).

---

## Break-Even With Founder Salary

**Fixed costs including founder:** $8,400 + $36,000 = $44,400 (illustrative Year 2 founder salary).

**Break-even revenue:** $44,400 / 0.25 = **$177,600** annual revenue, or **$14,800 per month** sustained.

**In site terms:** At $2,800 per site per month, **~5.3 sites** (effectively 5–6 sites) are required to break even with a $36,000 founder salary and $8,400 other overhead. If overhead is higher (e.g. $28,000 total before founder), then $28,000 + $36,000 = $64,000; $64,000 / 0.25 = $256,000 revenue, or about **9 sites** at $2,800/month.

## Sensitivity: 10% Revenue Drop

Assume base case Year 1 revenue $54,400 drops 10% to **$48,960**.

| Item | Base | With 10% revenue drop |
|------|------|------------------------|
| Revenue | 54,400 | 48,960 |
| COGS (75%) | 40,800 | 36,720 |
| Gross profit | 13,600 | 12,240 |
| Overhead | 8,400 | 8,400 |
| Net P&L | 5,200 | 3,840 |

Profit drops by about $1,360; company remains profitable but with thinner cushion. Cash flow is also weaker (less cash in).

---

## Sensitivity: 5% Margin Compression

Gross margin falls from 25% to **20%** (e.g. higher labour cost or price concession). COGS = 80% of revenue.

| Item | Base (25%) | 20% margin (same revenue) |
|------|------------|----------------------------|
| Revenue | 54,400 | 54,400 |
| COGS | 40,800 | 43,520 |
| Gross profit | 13,600 | 10,880 |
| Overhead | 8,400 | 8,400 |
| Net P&L | 5,200 | 2,480 |

Profit drops by $2,720. Break-even revenue at 20% margin = $8,400 / 0.20 = $42,000 (vs. $33,600 at 25%). More revenue is needed to cover the same overhead when margin compresses.

## Sensitivity: 30-Day AR Delay

Revenue is unchanged in amount but cash is received 30 days later. Effect: **liquidity**, not P&L. In Month 5, for example, revenue might be $2,800 but cash in might be $1,400 (from Month 4) + partial Month 5. Cumulative cash position is worse in early months; company may need **working capital or LOC** to pay COGS and overhead before receipts. A 30-day delay on $54,400 annual revenue implies roughly **$4,500–$5,500** of extra cash need at any point (one month of revenue). A 45-day delay implies roughly **$6,800–$8,200** extra cash need. See Section 13 for LOC sizing.

## Stress Scenario: Combined Shocks

Assume: (1) 10% revenue drop, (2) 5% margin compression (20% margin), (3) 45-day AR.

| Item | Value |
|------|--------|
| Revenue | 48,960 |
| COGS (80%) | 39,168 |
| Gross profit | 9,792 |
| Overhead | 8,400 |
| Net P&L | 1,392 |

Company remains marginally profitable but cash flow is severely stressed: less cash in, later. Working capital or LOC is essential in this scenario to avoid missing payroll or supplier payments.

---

## Stress Scenario Commentary

- **Revenue risk:** Slow ramp or loss of one site materially reduces revenue; break-even is then harder to reach.
- **Margin risk:** Labour cost increase or price pressure compresses margin; more revenue required to cover fixed costs.
- **Liquidity risk:** AR delay or one-time expense (e.g. equipment, legal) increases cash need; LOC or reserves mitigate.
- **No founder salary in Year 1** reduces fixed cost and extends runway; introducing founder salary increases break-even revenue as shown above.

Sensitivity and stress cases are not predictions; they illustrate how the plan behaves under adverse conditions and support the case for conservative planning and working capital facility.

## Sensitivity: Maintenance Mix (Blended Margin Improvement)

If maintenance grows to **25% of revenue** (with maintenance at 32% margin and cleaning at 25%), blended gross margin improves.

**Base (cleaning-heavy):** 100% cleaning at 25% margin → blended = **25%**.

**Mix shift:** 75% cleaning (25% margin) + 25% maintenance (32% margin).

- Blended margin = (0.75 × 25%) + (0.25 × 32%) = 18.75% + 8% = **26.75%** (round to **27–28%** with other small categories).

So: as maintenance reaches 25% of revenue, blended margin increases from 25% to approximately **27–28%**, all else equal. This is modeled explicitly so that growth in higher-margin maintenance is reflected in margin targets and does not rely on optimism; the improvement is mathematical given the tier margin assumptions in Section 09.

## 20% Recession Stress Test

A severe but plausible stress: 20% site loss, 5% price discount on remaining sites, add-ons down 30%, AR stretches to 60 days. The test checks whether the model remains viable.

### Assumptions

| Shock | Assumption |
|-------|------------|
| Site loss | 20% of sites lost (e.g. non-renewal or client exit). |
| Price on remaining | 5% discount on remaining contract revenue. |
| Add-ons | Decline 30% (recleans, extras). |
| AR | Stretches to 60 days (liquidity strain). |

### Example: 20 sites → 16 sites

| Item | Before shock | After shock |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| Sites | 20 | 16 |
| Monthly revenue (at $2,800/site) | 56,000 | 44,800 |
| After 5% discount | — | 42,560 |
| COGS (80% — compressed margin) | — | 34,048 |
| Gross profit | — | 8,512 |
| Overhead (fixed, e.g. $8,000) | 8,000 | 8,000 |
| **Net P&L (monthly)** | — | **512** |

**Conclusion:** The company remains marginally positive in this scenario. At higher overhead (e.g. founder salary), net could be negative; the key is that **no heavy payroll burden** and **subcontractor model flexibility** allow COGS to fall with revenue, and **low fixed overhead** limits the burn. AR at 60 days increases working capital need; LOC or cash buffer is essential. The model is recession-resilient provided margin governance and cost structure are maintained.
