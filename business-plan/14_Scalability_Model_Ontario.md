# Scalability Model — Ontario

## Blvckshell — Facilities Execution and Accountability Operator

---

## Multi-Service Impact on Scale

The multi-service model (cleaning, turnovers, audits, light maintenance, facilities support) affects scalability in two ways.

**Upside:** (1) **Revenue per site** increases when a site has recurring cleaning plus turnover, maintenance, or audits. (2) **Stickiness** improves: clients with multiple service lines are harder to replace. (3) **Retention** benefits from single-point accountability and documentation. (4) **LTV** increases with blended revenue per site. (5) **Exit multiple** can improve for a facilities execution operator with diversified revenue and margin discipline versus a cleaning-only operator.

**Cost of scale:** (1) **Supervision requirements** increase: maintenance and audits need scope and quality oversight in addition to cleaning QA. (2) **Insurance** must explicitly cover light maintenance and be reviewed as scope grows. (3) **Maintenance-trained subcontractors** are required; recruitment and vetting are more selective than cleaning-only.

Scaling assumptions below use a blended revenue-per-site figure; as maintenance and other tiers grow, revenue per site can rise and blended margin can improve (see Section 09, 12).

---

## Capacity Framework

Scalability is modeled using **team capacity math**: productive hours per worker per month, hours per site per month (from visit frequency and visit length), and therefore **sites per FTE**. From that, **teams required** and **revenue** are derived for 1, 3, 7, and 15 cities. Assumptions are conservative (no aggressive productivity assumptions).

---

## Base Assumptions (Consistent Across Scales)

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|--------|
| Productive hours per FTE per month | 110 |
| Average hours per site per month | 22 (e.g. ~2 visits/week × 2.5 hours per visit) |
| Sites per FTE | 110 / 22 ≈ **5** (rounded; conservative) |
| Monthly revenue per site | $2,800 |
| Gross margin | 25% |

**Buildings per city:** Assumed **40–80** addressable buildings per city (conservative; see Section 03). Blvckshell does not need to serve all; it needs enough to build density in each chosen city.

## Management Layer Inflection Points (Narrative)

- **1 city, 4–8 sites:** Founder-led or single coordinator; subcontractors report to one point. Simple.
- **3 cities:** One person cannot effectively schedule, QA, and manage clients across three geographies. Need at least 0.5–1 FTE ops/coordination; systems (checklists, evidence, billing) become essential.
- **7 cities:** Dedicated operations role; possible regional or city-level leads; admin and systems support. Without this layer, quality and margin suffer.
- **15 cities:** Multi-layer organization; finance and reporting must be investor- and bank-ready. This is the scale at which exit optionality (sale, recap) becomes realistic if execution and margins are strong.

All scaling assumes **contract-first** and **density within each city**; no city is opened without contracts and no city is scaled without capacity. Multi-service delivery increases revenue per site and retention but also requires maintenance-capable labour and clear risk controls (Section 08).
